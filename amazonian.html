The diets

Plant diets — sanango, Capirona, Bobinsana, the palos — are the core of this work. They are not psychoactive. They are protocols: a strict combination of plant preparation, isolation, dietary restriction, and behavioral discipline that the Amazonian tradition has refined over centuries.

The Diet is a ceremonial process, in the proper, traditional sense of that word. It does not begin when you arrive and end when you leave. The maestro and his family prepare it by hand — walking the forest to collect the roots and the tree bark, then preparing each plant the way their lineage has always prepared it. Nothing about it is industrial. It is slow, deliberate, and old.

For the duration of the Diet you hold a strict set of restrictions — in what you eat, in how you live, in what you keep away from. This is not arbitrary discipline. The restriction is what lets the plant do its work; the boundaries are part of the medicine. The maestro opens your Diet with an icaro, the sung medicine of his tradition, and it is his icaros that hold and direct the work throughout. When the Diet is complete, he closes it with a major icaro, and you conclude the process in the traditional way — by taking salt, which formally lifts the restrictions and seals what the plant has done. There is a beginning, a middle, and a clear, ceremonial end.

Each plant works differently. Sanango roots out what is lodged at the cellular level — memory, pattern, chronic tension. Bobinsana opens what has been closed, particularly around the heart and the capacity to receive. The palos, the tree barks, work slowly and structurally — they are Don Alberto’s specific lineage as a Palero, a master of tree medicines. What they have in common is that they are not an experience you have and return from. They build something in you that wasn’t there before.

The deeper traditional work, if a client is called to it, comes afterward — as a conclusion to a process already well underway, and entirely in the family’s hands. Some are. Some aren’t. The Diets carry the work either way.

What actually changes

The people who come to this work are not broken. They are self-aware, capable, and have already done more inner work than most people ever will. What they are looking for is not repair. It is access to a layer of themselves that thinking alone cannot reach.

The Diets, when approached properly, do not give you answers. They give you direct access to questions you didn’t know you were carrying. The patterns running your relationships, your decisions, your sense of what is possible — they become visible in a way they simply aren’t in ordinary life.

What you do with that visibility is the work — and it doesn’t happen in the Amazon. It happens when you get home, to the same environment, the same peers, the same defaults, with a new reality in your hands and no one around who understands it. That is the moment almost everyone fails. They feel the pull of the old life, the fear of the new one, and they quietly slide back — then book the next thing to feel it again.

That moment is a choice: default back to the life you already know, or actually live the healthier one you just glimpsed. You don’t make that choice once — you make it every week for a year. That is exactly where I stay with you, and where the community stays with you. It is the thing the journey, on its own, can never give you. For the people who do this seriously, it shifts something they had stopped believing could move: what they tolerate, how they decide, what they finally give themselves permission to do.
