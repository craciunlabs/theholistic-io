The part most people skip

The deep work isn't a peak you chase. It's the slow part most people skip.

The Diet is profound, and it's real. But the work doesn't fix you — the year after does. The journey gives you visibility. What you do with it once you're home is the actual work, and it's where almost everyone quietly slides back: same environment, same peers, same defaults, and no one around who understands what just happened.

So they book the next peak to feel it again. The choice isn't made once — it's made every week, for a year.

I hold the line with you across the whole arc. Before you arrive, while you're there, and for the twelve months after. The community holds it with you too. That slow integration at home is the part most people skip — and the part the journey, on its own, can never give you.

A path, not a menu

The Diet isn't the first step for most people. Often what's needed is one honest conversation that cuts through the noise — which is what a Clarity Session is for. For many, that's enough: the pattern gets named, the next move gets clear, and they go do the work with what they already have.

The Diet is for when that isn't enough — when you've had the clarity, you've done the practice, and the thing still won't move. The natural next step, not the first one. I'll tell you honestly which one you're ready for.

About Claudiu

Not a guru.
Not a shaman.
A man who went to the bottom — and found his way back.

I’d built a life that looked fine from the outside. Underneath it I was lost, and I’d run out of things to try. The books, the teachers, the next person with the next answer — I treated myself like a problem to solve, because solving things was what I knew how to do. It worked for a while. Then the same patterns came back, exactly as they were.

There came a point where I genuinely couldn’t see a reason to keep going.

Peru was my last hope. Not one option among others — the last one. I went not knowing if it would help, only that I had nothing left to lose and nothing left to try. What I found there wasn’t another answer. It was myself. And the slow, unglamorous work that came after, alongside Don Alberto Torres Dávila, a maestro of 50+ years in the Ucayali region of Peru, is what actually changed anything.

I’ve gone back many times since. Not for the peak — for the part that comes after, which is the part that lasts.

Now I sit with people doing the same searching I did, often standing somewhere near where I once stood. I’m not here to fix anyone. I’m here to walk alongside — one step ahead on the same trail.

Based in Hong Kong. There’s enormous need for this work here, and almost no door for it. I want to be one.

Romanian. Two decades in technology and business sit quietly behind the work.
